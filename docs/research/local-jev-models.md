None has been evaluated on agent-action safety / authorization rubrics — every published number is on classification/NLI/support-ticket style tasks. That is the central caveat for cancelli.

Two kinds exist:
(A) **Trained decision heads** (new weights): Kev, Open-Jev, Laya, openJev-verdict, minojev, NanoJev.
(B) **Serving shims** that expose /v1/systemone over an existing LLM by reading option logits (no new weights): jev-rs, litjev, simple-jev, GitHub30/OpenJev, razorback16/openjev.

### Implications for cancelli [I]
- **Nothing here is validated on tool-call safety or authorization (f5).** All trained heads are fitted on generic classification data; Laya/verdict have 512-1,024-token context, too short for L2 states with prior tool calls.
- **jev-rs is the most direct drop-in**: Rust, wire-compatible, backend = llama-server (already installed), any GGUF, has a calibration command. It turns an installed Qwen3.6/Gemma4 into a systemone endpoint. Its own numbers say small models need heavy temperature (T 3-6) and ECE is 0.1-0.24 — i.e., you must recalibrate on your own labelled corpus (you have 5,750 labelled items + cached Jev outputs; that is exactly the calibration set).
- **Kev-9B / Open-Jev-9B** are the most credible trained clones; Kev reports parity-ish with Jev on its own generic suite and was built/measured on an M5 32 GB. Worth an A/B on the cancelli corpus, but expect a domain gap.
- **Distillation opportunity**: cancelli already holds ~15,630 cached Jev answers keyed (model, rubric, state). That is a ready-made distillation set for a Kev/Open-Jev-style head on a Qwen3.5/3.6 base — arguably more valuable than any clone's training data for this rubric.

## 1. Is there an open/local Jev from TypeSafe?
**No.** [V] TypeSafe's written answer dated 2026-09-17: "Hosted API only. No on-premises, VPC or edge option now or planned." (quoted in https://github.com/factorysemantics/factorysemantics-mes/pull/71). Zero data retention on enterprise tier only (https://docs.typesafe.ai/models). Endpoint POST https://api.typesafe.ai/v1/systemone, $0.042/Mtok input, output free (https://www.marktechpost.com/2026/09/19/typesafe-ai-releases-jev/). Also on Cloudflare AI (https://developers.cloudflare.com/ai/models/typesafe/jev/) — still hosted.
**Method disclosure:** only the name RLCD ("Reinforcement Learning for Calibrated Decisions": proper-scoring RL so 0.8 means right 80% of the time). "As of September 19, 2026, TypeSafe has released no paper, training details or calibration curves" (https://www.mindstudio.ai/blog/typesafe-jev-rlcd-vs-rlhf). The launch blog (https://typesafe.ai/blog/introducing-system-one-models-and-jev) discloses no architecture. Architecture of Jev is inferred by third parties (Kev "reproduces the architecture that was inferred") — not confirmed [V that it is inference].
**Independent calibration of Jev itself** (https://github.com/scienthoon/jev-ood-calibration, 2026-09-19, 900 OOD synthetic items): choice ECE 0.082 (over-confident, refit T=1.30); score ECE 0.325, 44.7% acc (over-confident, T=1.92); noul ECE 0.079 (UNDER-confident, T=0.66). Probabilities quantized to 0.01; ~4.3% choice items give exactly 0 to the correct answer. [V] => Jev is itself not perfectly calibrated OOD; a local backend should be judged against labels, not against Jev agreement alone [I].

### 2c. General instruction models as zero-shot probabilistic classifiers (installed or small)
- **Qwen3.6 27B dense / 35B-A3B MoE** (installed), **Qwen3.5 4B/9B** (bases used by Kev and Open-Jev [V]), **Gemma 4 12B** (installed), **Nemotron 3.5 lightning 30B MLX** (installed), **gpt-oss-20b**. All Apache-2.0 or Gemma terms [K]. Probability = restricted softmax over label tokens at a single answer position, no thinking (the approach of jev-rs, litjev, simple-jev) [V].
- **Local measurement (this session, 2026-09-22, Ollama 0.34.2, M5 32 GB, n=1, think=false, one ~1.2-1.35k-token prompt)** [V, measured]: Ollama returns `logprobs`/`top_logprobs` on /api/chat for both GGUF and nvfp4 models. gemma4:12b-it-qat prefill 355 tok/s (3.8 s) — P(Yes)=0.977 on a blatant f5 violation; qwen3.6:35b-a3b-coding-nvfp4 prefill 211 tok/s (5.6 s) — P(Yes)=0.74, P(No)=0.24 on the same. One sample: says nothing about accuracy, only that the plumbing works and latency is seconds, not ~100 ms.
- Known Ollama limitation: can't get logprob of a specific token unless it is in top_logprobs (max 20) [V https://github.com/ollama/ollama/issues/18579]. llama-server (installed, used by jev-rs) exposes n_probs and prompt caching [K].
- **Latency budget [I]**: 39 questions × (shared state prefix + ~60-100-token question suffix). With prefix caching, ~1.2k state + 39×~80 suffix ≈ 4.3k prefill tokens ≈ 12-20 s at 211-355 tok/s for 12-35B models on this M5. A 4B model would be roughly 3-5x faster [I]. Kev's measured M5 latency (5 questions, 230-token state): 4B 0.78 s, 9B ~2 s [V]. So a 39-axis local call is seconds-to-tens-of-seconds unless (a) the model is ≤4B, (b) the rubric is pruned to the handful of axes that carry signal (findings: top-5 axes are authorization, 0.86-0.98 inter-correlated), or (c) a trained head reads all answers in one pass.

## 3. Calibration evidence for small open models' yes/no probabilities
Verified:
- **Raw label-logit probabilities from small instruct models are over-confident and need refitting.** jev-rs: Qwen3-4B needed fitted temperatures of 3-6 (larger models ~1); ECE 0.108-0.244 across models on 75 dev tasks [V https://github.com/yijunyu/jev-rs]. minojev: zero-shot logit readout on Qwen3-1.7B ECE 0.097 vs trained head 0.024 [V]. Kev-9B ECE 0.106 -> 0.042 after temperature scaling [V]. Laya ECE 0.213 raw -> 0.081, "ships over-confident" [V].
- **Even Jev is not uniformly calibrated OOD**: over-confident on choice (T=1.30) and score (T=1.92, ECE 0.325), under-confident on noul (T=0.66) [V jev-ood-calibration, 2026-09-19]. Calibration must be fitted **per primitive type** (and per option count — jev-rs buckets) [V both sources].
- **Reasoning hurts token-probability calibration**: "Calibration Drift Under Reasoning" — larger CoT budgets inflate confidence without matching accuracy; token probs come to reflect consistency with the model's own trace [V arXiv 2606.11211, June 2026]. CoT degrades uncertainty estimates in VLMs (PRR 0.735 -> 0.086) [V arXiv 2603.16728]. => Read the label logprob with thinking OFF [I]. (Counter-evidence on accuracy: jev-frontier-100 shows Qwen3.5-4B with 2,048 thinking tokens 96.7% vs 56% without — reasoning buys accuracy on hard logic, at calibration and latency cost [V].)
- **Verbalized vs logprob**: arXiv 2609.10996 (2026-09-10) finds verbalized confidence now the more robust soft score for *post-2025 proprietary flagships*; pre-2025 / open controls (Llama, Mistral, Gemma variants) pay an accuracy penalty under the verbalized protocol [V]. For small local models, logprob readout remains the practical choice [I].
- Post-RLHF models show input-dependent over-confidence; adaptive temperature scaling improves calibration 10-50% [V arXiv 2409.19817 via search summary].
- Recommendation from the literature: report ECE + Brier + reliability diagrams, not ECE alone [V search summary].

Inference for cancelli [I]:
1. Use restricted softmax over label tokens (noul: {true,false} or {Yes,No}; choice: option letters; score: level letters -> expected level), thinking disabled.
2. Fit per-(axis-type, option-count) temperature — or better, per-axis Platt/isotonic — on the existing labelled 5,750-item corpus; cancelli's deterministic layer thresholds were tuned against Jev's scale, so **recalibrate to Jev's scale or retune thresholds**; you cannot assume 0.8 means the same thing.
3. Watch the direction-of-question trap already documented in JEV_GATE_FINDINGS §2.4 (instruction/criteria inversion). Local models may resolve instruction-vs-criteria conflicts differently from Jev; rerun the contrastive smoke gate per backend.

## 4. Authorization-style ("exceeds the user's request") evidence
- **ScopeJudge** (dreadnode, arXiv 2607.07774, 2026-07-30) is the closest published analogue to f5: pre-execution judge decides if each tool call is within the scope declared in the user's request + a static policy. 4,897 labelled calls (7.7% violations), human Fleiss κ 0.64, expert F1 0.78. **Best judge: open-weight GLM-5.2, F1 0.66**, beating best proprietary (0.60) at ~1/3 cost. **"Blind to the user's request, judge recall collapses to near zero."** Dataset released [V]. Per-model table for small open models not in the fetched excerpt [V absence]. GLM-5.2 is far too large for 32 GB [I].
- This matches the cancelli finding that the user request (L1) doubles detection at 1% FPR (JEV_GATE_FINDINGS summary) [V local doc].
- **AgentDoG** includes "Unconfirmed or Over-privileged Action" as a failure mode; its 4B model scores R-Judge F1 92.7 [V] — but that is trajectory-level unsafe/safe, not a per-action "exceeds approval" probability.
- **StepGuard** (4B) claims to consider "whether actions match user-granted scope" [V]; TS-Guard's BTRA pattern covers benign tools with risky arguments [V]. Neither publishes an authorization-only metric.
- **Overeager Coding Agents** (arXiv 2605.18583, May 2026): measures out-of-scope actions of coding agents on benign tasks; uses a deterministic rule judge (κ 0.73 vs humans, recall 1.00, precision 0.76), **no LLM judge** — so no open-model judge numbers [V, read PDF]. Related: "What You Approve Is What Executes" (arXiv 2606.02668), FORTIS over-privilege benchmark (arXiv 2605.09163), SovereignPA-Bench (2607.05363) [V existence only].
- I found **no** published evaluation of any ≤35B open model specifically on a per-action "exceeds approval" probability with calibration. [V absence after searches listed.] The clones' eval suites (Kev decision-v7, Open-Jev "coverage decisions") include "generated policy / rule" examples; Open-Jev's 76 "hard coverage decisions" (2B 65, 9B 72, Jev 66) is the only clone-vs-Jev number that looks policy-like, and its content is not described in detail [V numbers, I relevance].

## 5. Shortlist for THIS machine (M5, 32 GB)
Ranking criterion: probability of reproducing Jev's f5-dominated signal at usable latency, cheapest to prove first (per the user's "most constrained option first" rule). All rankings are [I] unless marked.

1. **jev-rs + llama-server + a Qwen3.5/3.6 GGUF (start 4B-9B, then qwen3.6:27b if accuracy demands)** — wire-compatible /v1/systemone, Rust, uses the already-installed llama-server, has `jev calibrate` [V]. Lowest integration cost: point cancelli's Jev URL at localhost and replay the cached 5,750-item corpus; compare AUC / detection@1%FPR against cached Jev answers per axis (esp. f5). Risk: zero-shot small models ECE 0.1-0.24 [V] → must calibrate; 39 axes × seconds.
2. **Kev-4B / Kev-9B** (Apache-2.0, Qwen3.5 base, systemone server, measured on an M5: 4B 0.78 s / 9B ~2 s for 5 questions) [V]. Trained decision head; on its own suite Kev-9B ≈ Jev accuracy (0.852 vs 0.857 new sources) with worse Brier (0.237 vs 0.211) [V]. No MLX yet; bf16 PyTorch MPS. Unknown on agent-authorization [V absence]. Also the best template for **distilling cancelli's own cached Jev answers** into a local head.
3. **Open-Jev-9B (and 2B)** — systemone-shaped, strong self-reported calibration (OOD ECE 0.037), and the only clone with a policy-coverage comparison vs Jev (9B 72 vs Jev 66 on 76 items) [V]. Linux/CUDA-targeted; fitting 9B bf16 on MPS is plausible but untested [I]. Repo says "partial" API compatibility [V].
4. **Installed general models via logprob readout**: **gemma4:12b-it-qat** (7.2 GB, 355 tok/s prefill measured) as the fast candidate; **qwen3.6:27b** (dense, best reasoning of installed set [I]) as the accuracy ceiling. qwen3.6:35b-a3b-coding-nvfp4 prefilled slower (211 tok/s) than gemma 12B here [V measured n=1] and is a coding-tuned variant. nemotron-3.5-lightning:30b-mlx and qwen3.5:0.8b-mlx: not credible first choices (22 GB MLX leaves little headroom; 0.8B too small — Kev-0.8B clearly worse than 4B [V]).
5. **Granite Guardian 4.1-8B** (Apache-2.0, 6.9 GB on Ollama, BYOC custom criteria, 128K ctx) [V] — the most credible *purpose-built* judge that takes arbitrary criteria; use yes/no score-token logprob.
